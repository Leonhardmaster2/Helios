Helios

Nothing here is painted.

A real-time Vulkan engine in which every nebula, galaxy, pulsar, black hole and planet is the live state of a simulation or an exact light path through a physical model. The arms, pillars, shells and beams are what the physics made. The only things anyone chose are the starting conditions.

N-body, 900 million years in

Disk galaxy

Two million star and gas particles, gravity solved on a mesh, started as a smooth, featureless disk in equilibrium. Nobody drew the arms: swing-amplified density waves grew out of the particle noise, a bar formed, and the gas piled into the arms and turned into stars there. The colour is the age of the stars.

Emission nebula, pillars

A turbulent cloud of 900 atoms per cubic centimetre and a 55 solar-mass star placed inside it. Photo-ionisation and a MUSCL, HLLC solver run in compute shaders; the dense clumps shadow the gas behind them and are carved into pillars with bright rims, as the Eagle Nebula's were.

Emission nebula, H II region

The same solver with a 40 solar-mass star in a thinner cloud. The ionisation front races out, the heated gas pushes a cavity open, and the thin medium around the grid scatters the star's light instead of ending at a wall. The solver is checked against Sod's shock tube and the Sedov–Taylor blast wave.

Supernova remnant, pulsar-wind dominated

Five solar masses of expanding ejecta, then a pulsar switched on in the middle. Its wind inflates a bubble that overtakes the ejecta from inside; Rayleigh–Taylor fingers grow at the contact, as in the Crab. The bubble shines by synchrotron, the shocked shell by Balmer and forbidden lines.

Modelled on
the Crab Nebula, M1
Other presets
core-collapse with pulsar, Type Ia in thin gas (Tycho), an old radiative shell (Cygnus Loop)

Exact light paths, one rotation

Pulsar

Light paths integrated through Schwarzschild spacetime around a magnetised star turning twenty times a second, its magnetic axis almost at right angles to its spin. The ring of smeared stars is the sky behind, bent around the star by its gravity. The dark belt is the current sheet of its wind, seen edge-on at the light cylinder.

Computed
geodesics, dipole magnetosphere, Goldreich–Julian outflow, split-monopole current sheet
States
young, millisecond, orthogonal, nearly aligned; magnetar and accreting for the neutron star
Bands
visible light or the X-ray band

Black hole

Schwarzschild geodesics from every pixel. The arch over the shadow is the far side of the disk, lensed over the top; the ring inside is light that went around the hole once. The brighter side is the one coming towards you, beamed and blueshifted by its own motion.

From a billion metres to eye level

Earth-like world

A height field with the statistics of real terrain, evaluated identically on the CPU and the GPU down to six-metre cells, so the camera can descend from orbit to the ground without a loading screen. Above it an atmosphere scattered twice over, a cloud layer marched through, and a moon on its orbit.

A planetary system

Ringed gas giant

Rings as a single-scattering slab whose gaps sit where the moons' resonances put them. The planet's shadow falls across the rings and theirs across the planet's southern hemisphere; four icy moons orbit with the mean motion the planet's mass gives them.

Every preset, captured at 4K

Each object has named presets after real classes of object: a set of initial conditions, or disk parameters, and a view that shows it. These are the engine's own 3840 by 2160 captures of all thirty, forty-eight jittered frames averaged each. Click one to see it full size.

How it is computed

C++20 on Vulkan through Diligent Engine, shaders in Slang compiled offline. Each object is its own model with its own solver; one display chain averages still frames, adds glare, and tone-maps all of them the same way.

Gas dynamics

MUSCL reconstruction, HLLC fluxes and SSP Runge–Kutta stepping in compute shaders, on a grid stretched so its cells are finest at the centre. Photo-ionisation, radiative cooling of swept-up gas, and a pulsar-wind source where the recipe has one. The display continues the thin medium beyond the grid analytically, so no face of the box is ever visible.

N-body galaxy

Particle-mesh gravity: cloud-in-cell deposit, FFT convolution with the softened kernel, leapfrog steps, on its own CPU thread. The disk starts in equilibrium at Toomre Q of 1.3; arms and bar grow from the particle noise, and gas forms stars where it piles up, with feedback pushing back.

Exact light paths

Schwarzschild geodesics integrated per pixel for the neutron star, the pulsar and the black hole, with Doppler beaming and gravitational redshift applied to every emitting element along the ray, in visible light or the X-ray band.

Cube-sphere terrain

A quadtree of tiles chosen by screen-space error, camera-relative double precision on the CPU and patch-local lattice anchors on the GPU, so octave 22 of the noise is exact at eye level from a camera that started a billion metres away.

Sky, clouds, rings

Single scattering along the ray plus a multiple-scattering table after Hillaire 2020. Clouds as a drifting cover field eroded by billows, lit by the two-stream reflectance of a conservative slab. Rings as a slab with Lindblad gaps, shadowing and shadowed.

Presets and quality

Named presets after real classes of object, each with a view that shows it. A quality preset sets grid size, ray-march samples, particle counts and level of detail: Performance, Balanced, Ultra.

What is assumed rather than computed

The honest list. Everything here is a choice; everything above follows from the physics.

Initial conditions. The galaxy's mass model, the cloud's size and turbulence, the explosion's energy, the planet's hypsometry. Nothing that follows from them is prescribed.

Brightness scales. Where no absolute calibration is carried through, the relative strength of emission processes is set by hand: scattered starlight in the nebula, bremsstrahlung and synchrotron in the remnant, the neutron star's non-thermal components. The spatial pattern of each is computed.

Resolution. Ionisation fronts, the knots that cooled ejecta collapse into, and the radiative shell of an old remnant are smaller than a cell and are represented by a floor or a closure named in the source.

The planet. Its relief has the statistics of real terrain but is not a simulation of the processes that shape one; the cloud base is a height above the sea level; moons cast no eclipses yet.

The gas objects run their simulation on the GPU in the same frame, within a five-millisecond budget; the galaxy integrates on a CPU thread and the renderer draws whatever state is newest. At eye level under a cloud deck the Earth-like world costs 22 ms (45 fps) at 4K: the cloud march dominates there.

Ultra draws the Earth-like world from orbit at 3.9 ms (255 fps) and runs the nebula on a 320³ grid at 12.8 ms; Performance runs it on 192³ at 4.5 ms.

Build and run it

Windows, Visual Studio 2022 and a Vulkan 1.3 GPU. Dependencies are pinned and fetched by a script; Diligent Engine is a submodule.

git clone --recursive https://github.com/Proceduralabs/Helios.git
cd Helios
node scripts/fetch_deps.mjs --update-lock --write-cmake
cmake --preset windows-dev
cmake --build --preset windows-dev
ctest --preset windows-dev
helios_deep_sky_lab --object galaxy
helios_deep_sky_lab --object nebula --variant pillars --capture pillars.png
helios_deep_sky_lab --object planet --variant ringed-gas-giant --benchmark

1 to 7 switch objects, Space pauses, R resets the view, the mouse orbits.

The lab never grabs the mouse or reads input while unfocused. The capture and benchmark routes create no window at all; every frame on this page came out of them at 3840 by 2160, and none was retouched.

Every model cites its sources in the code: Bucholtz 1995, Goldreich and Tremaine 1978, Meador and Weaver 1980, Hillaire 2020, Page and Thorne 1974, Shakura and Sunyaev 1973, Schneider and Vos 2015, among others.
